# Job search plan

Treat the search like a part-time job with a steady weekly routine. Consistency beats bursts of
effort.

## Weekly routine

| Activity | Target per week | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Tailored applications | 10–15 | Tailor the top third of your resume to the job's keywords |
| Referral and networking messages | 5–10 | Referrals get far more callbacks than cold applications |
| Coding practice | 5 sessions | See `INTERVIEW_PREP.md` |
| Portfolio work | 2–3 hours | Ship small improvements to CampusMatch and keep CI green |
| Follow-ups | Every open thread more than 7 days old | One polite nudge, then move on |

Log everything in `application_tracker.csv`. Every two weeks, look at your conversion rates:

- **Few responses to applications** → fix the resume or target better-fitting roles.
- **Screens but no onsites** → practice the pitch and coding fundamentals.
- **Onsites but no offers** → do mock interviews and work on behavioral stories.

## Resume checklist

- [ ] One page, a simple single-column layout, exported as PDF. Applicant tracking systems
      handle this format best.
- [ ] Header: name, email, phone, city, GitHub, LinkedIn, and portfolio link if you have one.
- [ ] Order: Education (if in school or recently graduated) → Experience → Projects → Skills.
- [ ] Each bullet starts with a strong verb, then what you did, how, and the result with a
      number.
- [ ] No "responsible for", no paragraphs, no photo, no "References available upon request".
- [ ] The skills section lists only things you could be quizzed on.
- [ ] CampusMatch is listed with a link and 2–3 bullets from `PROJECT_PITCH.md`.
- [ ] Tailored for each application: mirror the job description's keywords where they're true.
- [ ] Spell-checked, with consistent date formats and verb tenses.

## GitHub and LinkedIn

- [ ] Pin CampusMatch and 1–3 other projects on GitHub. Each needs a README with a screenshot.
- [ ] Add a GitHub profile README: one line about you, what you're looking for, and links.
- [ ] LinkedIn headline describes the role you want, not just your current status. Example:
      "Software Engineer | Python, APIs, Algorithms | Open to new-grad roles".
- [ ] LinkedIn About section: 3–4 sentences covering what you build, what you're good at, and
      what you're looking for.
- [ ] Add CampusMatch to LinkedIn's Featured section with the screenshot.
- [ ] Turn on "Open to Work". It can be visible to recruiters only.

## Message templates

**Referral request (to an alum or someone at the company):**
> Hi <Name>, I'm <you>, a <year/major> at <school> (or "a software engineer based in <city>"). I
> saw you work on <team/product> at <Company>, and I'm applying for the <Role> position
> (<link>). I recently built <one-line project description>, which lines up with <something from
> the job description>. Would you be open to a 15-minute chat, or to referring me if it seems
> like a fit? Thanks either way!

**Cold message to a recruiter or hiring manager:**
> Hi <Name>, I just applied for <Role> (<req ID>). Quick summary: <one strong fact, e.g. "I built
> a matching service in Python/FastAPI with a verified implementation of Irving's stable
> roommates algorithm">. I'd love to be considered. Happy to share more. Thank you!

**Follow-up after 7–10 days:**
> Hi <Name>, following up on my application for <Role>. I'm still very interested, especially
> in <specific thing>. Is there anything else I can share?

**Thank-you after an interview:**
> Thanks for taking the time today, <Name>. I enjoyed talking about <specific topic>. It made me
> even more excited about <team/role>. Looking forward to hearing about next steps.

## Where to look

- Company career pages, filtered by "new grad", "entry level", "junior" or "associate".
- LinkedIn Jobs, with the "past week" filter and alerts on.
- Your school's career center and Handshake, if you're a student or recent graduate.
- Community-maintained new-grad job lists on GitHub (search "new grad positions").
- Local tech meetups and career fairs. Talking to people in person still works.

## Offer stage

- Always ask for the offer in writing, including base salary, bonus, equity, start date and
  benefits.
- It's normal to ask for a few days to decide.
- Negotiate politely, using market data or a competing offer as the reason. The worst realistic
  answer is "no".
